# Evidence to public content

Start with allowed source material and the intended reader's question. Identify one defensible main claim and an action the reader can take. Use supplied examples and confirmed voice; do not invent experiences, mistakes, measurements or audience feedback to sound personal.

Classify each claim as observed, replayed, illustrative, inferred or proposed. Record source links and the version actually read. Keep conditions and limits next to consequential claims. Installed tools do not prove use; instructions do not prove effectiveness.

Choose excerpts, code and images deliberately. Keep credentials, raw configuration/logs, personal paths and third-party information outside the public package. A useful public example may use neutral fixtures if it is labeled as a replay or illustration.

Adapt structure and detail to the medium while preserving the claim and author's voice. Each output should advance a specific point; remove empty hype and engagement padding. Use visual rendering/mobile preview where the artifact requires it, and check that commands, screenshots and captions agree.

Example: a task handoff becomes an internal team runbook with steps and recovery paths, or a short public tutorial with one reproducible fixture. Neither can claim a reliability gain without an appropriate comparison.

Return the draft, sources and remaining factual/visual checks. Keep drafting, uploading and publishing states distinct and obey scoped authorization for external actions. Do not infer permission from completion of the draft.

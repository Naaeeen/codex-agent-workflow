# Academic writing: argument before template

Use the actual venue, article type, evidence and requested voice. This is optional
editorial guidance, not a required outline, contribution count or citation quota.
An experimental paper, survey, proposal and student comment have different jobs.

## Claims and evidence

- Identify what is established, inferred, hypothesized or planned. Keep those
  distinctions in the wording; do not add universal hedges to every sentence.
- Explain why the evidence supports the claim. A citation's existence does not
  establish relevance, causality, novelty or generalization.
- Describe the contribution precisely. Do not claim first/novel without the
  literature support needed for that claim; specificity often removes the need.
- Use numbers where they exist and matter. Do not invent measurements or force
  quantitative framing onto a qualitative result.

## Structure and voice

An abstract should communicate the actual problem, approach, important result
and scope in the venue's space. An introduction should lead the reader to the
research question; a broad importance paragraph is not mandatory. Related work
should compare relevant dimensions fairly, not manufacture a gap in every paper.
Methods and results need enough detail to assess the work. A conclusion should
state what the work establishes; a section-by-section recap is optional.

Use first or third person as requested or conventional for the venue. Passive
voice can preserve the topic, but name the actor when responsibility matters.
Keep terminology consistent and connect sentences through a clear line of
argument. Avoid decorative transitions, inflated importance and fixed paragraph
rhythms. There is no universal number of contributions, references or sentences.

## Revision check

Read each paragraph for its point, evidence and reasoning link. Remove repetition
or claims the evidence cannot carry. Preserve specific limitations that affect
interpretation; do not weaken supported results merely to sound cautious. Check
that title, abstract and conclusion describe the same supported work.

For line editing, use the meaning-first guidance in article-writing rather than
a phrase blacklist. Keep required citations and AI disclosure; natural prose is
not permission to conceal authorship or fabricate personal research experience.

# Meaning-first editing

Begin with the reader's task: what should they understand, decide or do? Identify
the actual claim, evidence and reasoning that connects them. Fix a missing link
or an unjustified claim before changing sentence rhythm.

Prefer identifiable actors and useful verbs where the source supplies them.
Check how each sentence connects to the preceding topic; clarify vague this/it
references and topic jumps. Passive voice, headings, lists, repetition and
contrast can be useful. Do not mechanically remove them to satisfy a style rule.

Cut broad scene-setting, slogans and summary padding when they add no information.
Keep a qualification when removing it would change the claim. State a material
limitation once, where it matters; do not hedge an already established fact.

After revision compare original and revised facts, quantities, units, dates,
attributions, causal claims and certainty. Do not invent anecdotes, concrete
details, achievements, personal experience or mistakes to make prose sound human.
Natural writing need not be casual, first-person, short-sentence-only or imperfect.

Evaluate clarity with the reader's understanding when possible. A model style
judge, phrase count or AI detector is not evidence of human comprehension or
authorship. Use blind matched examples for a limited diagnostic, preserving ties
and factual regressions rather than manufacturing a preference.
